# RYA Competent Crew — syllabus research

Researched 2026-07-27. Every claim below is tagged with the URL of a page I actually opened and read (raw HTML fetched and stripped to text, not a search snippet). Where sources disagree I say so rather than picking one.

---

## 0. Two corrections to the brief before anything else

**0.1 — The Competent Crew syllabus is NOT in G4.**
G4 is the **RYA National Sailing Scheme Syllabus and Logbook** — dinghies, keelboats and multihulls (Level 1 Start Sailing, Level 2 Basic Skills, Level 3 Better Sailing, Seamanship Skills, Start Racing…). Product code literally "G4", £8.99.
Source opened: https://www.rya.org.uk/products/rya-national-sailing-scheme-syllabus-and-logbook/

The cruising logbook that contains Competent Crew is the **RYA Yachtmaster Scheme Syllabus and Logbook**, product code **G158**, £8.49, 128pp, edition 2015, reprinted 2025, ISBN 9781910017074. Its own blurb:

> "This book effectively combines, updates and replaces RYA Sail Cruising & Yachtmaster Scheme Syllabus and Logbook (G15) and RYA Motor Cruising & Yachtmaster Scheme Syllabus and Logbook (G18). It also introduces new courses and clarifies pre-exam requirements for all RYA Yachtmaster exams."

Source opened: https://www.rya.org.uk/products/rya-yachtmaster-scheme-syllabus-and-logbook/

So the lineage is **G15 (sail) + G18 (motor) → G158 (both)**. If you cite a book in the deck, cite G158, not G4. Almost every training-centre page reproducing the syllabus online is still reproducing the pre-2015 **G15** text.

**0.2 — "Environmental considerations" is not a top-level section.**
It exists, but as a sub-bullet inside **Manners and customs**. Confirmed on every full-syllabus source (Hamble, Sailtrain, Jolly Parrot, Nomad, Classic Sailing, Neilson, Endeavour). Do not give it its own deck section unless you deliberately want to.

## 1. The official section list

### 1a. What the RYA's own live page says (13 items)

From https://www.rya.org.uk/course-finder/competent-crew-practical-course/ — the "Course content" block, verbatim and in order:

1. Knowledge of sea terms and parts of a boat
2. Rigging and sails
3. Sail handling
4. Ropework
5. Fire precautions and fighting
6. Personal safety equipment
7. Man overboard
8. Emergency equipment
9. Meteorology
10. Seasickness
11. Helmsmanship
12. General duties
13. Manners

**Note the RYA's own marketing page splits "sea terms/parts of boat" from "rigging and sails" (which the logbook keeps as one section), and silently drops "Rules of the road" and "Dinghies".** This is a marketing summary, not the assessable syllabus. Do not build the deck from it.

Same page, course overview:
- **Course length:** "5 days, Often run over 3 weekends or 3 days plus a weekend. If you have done the Start Yachting course, this course can be completed in 3 or 4 days."
- **Course format:** "This course is based on the water."
- **Required experience:** "None"
- **Minimum age:** "Recommended age 12"
- **Assessment:** "Assessment is based on how well you have performed your practical and navigation tasks during your time on board." → **no written exam, continuous instructor assessment.**

## 2. Section-by-section table

Depth key:
- **DO** = assessed as a physical skill, hands-on, must perform it
- **KNOW** = must understand / be able to state, but not perform under pressure
- **AWARE** = named only, one-line exposure, explicitly shallower than Day Skipper
- **SELF** = a logbook status tick, not a competence

| # | Section (logbook wording) | What the candidate must know / do (verbatim bullets) | Depth | Deck implication |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Knowledge of sea terms and parts of a boat, her rigging and sails** | "Sufficient knowledge to understand orders given concerning the sailing and day to day running of the boat." | KNOW | Biggest card yield. Pure vocabulary: hull/rig/sail parts, points of sail, port/starboard, windward/leeward, sheet vs halyard, tack/gybe, forestay/backstay/shrouds, boom/kicker/topping lift, clew/tack/head/luff/leech/foot. **The bar is "understands an order", not "can explain the physics".** |
| 2 | **Sail handling** | "Bending on, setting, reefing and handling of sails." / "Use of sheets and halyards and their associated winches." | DO | Cards on procedure + terminology: bending on, hoisting, reefing (slab), winch use, riding turn, tailing, self-tailer. |
| 3 | **Ropework** | "Handling ropes, including coiling, stowing, securing to cleats and single and double bollards." / "Handling warps." / "Ability to tie the following knots and to know their correct use: figure-of-eight, clove hitch, rolling hitch, bowline, round turn and two half hitches, single and double sheet bend, reef knot." | DO | **7 named knots** (single and double sheet bend counted as one entry → 8 if you split them). Each needs a knot card + a "correct use" card. Plus cleating, coiling, warp names (bow/stern line, spring, breast line). Your brief's phrasing "understands the importance of neatness" does **not** appear in any source I opened — drop it. |
| 4 | **Fire precautions and fighting** | "Awareness of the hazards of fire and the precautions necessary to prevent fire." / "Knowledge of the action to be taken in the event of fire." | KNOW | Fire triangle, gas/fuel/galley/electrical hazards, extinguisher types and locations, fire blanket, gas bottle turn-off, action-on-fire sequence. |
| 5 | **Personal safety equipment** | "Understands and complies with rules for the wearing of safety harnesses, lifejackets and personal buoyancy aids." | KNOW + DO | Lifejacket vs buoyancy aid, newtons, crotch strap, harness + tether, jackstay, clip-on rules, when the skipper's rule applies. |
| 6 | **Man overboard** | "Understands the action to be taken to recover a man overboard." (Newer wording adds: "…and how cold water shock can affect a casualty in the water.") | KNOW | **Crew-side only:** shout "man overboard", point continuously, throw danbuoy/horseshoe, press MOB on the plotter, keep eyes on. **Not** the manoeuvre — that's the skipper's job at Day Skipper. |
| 7 | **Emergency equipment** | "Can operate distress flares and knows when they should be used." / "Understands how to launch and board a liferaft." | KNOW + DO | Flare types (red handheld, red parachute, orange smoke, white collision), how to hold one, liferaft painter/ties, "step up into the raft", grab bag. |
| 8 | **Manners and customs** | "Understands accepted practice with regard to: use of burgees and ensigns, prevention of unnecessary noise or disturbance in harbour including courtesies to other craft berthed [alongside]." / "Aware of the responsibility of yacht skippers to protect the environment." | KNOW | Ensign at the stern, ensign hours (0800–sunset), burgee, courtesy flag, halyard slap, rafting up — cross bow-to-bow, ask before crossing, run your own lines ashore. Environmental sub-bullet: no overboard rubbish, holding tanks, anti-foul, eelgrass, fuel spill. |
| 9 | **Rules of the road** | "Is able to keep an efficient lookout at sea." | **AWARE** | **This is the single line.** IRPCS Rule 5 lookout, and nothing else is assessable. See §3. |
| 10 | **Dinghies** (newer wording: **Tender usage**) | "Understands and complies with the loading rules." / "Is able to handle a dinghy under oars." (Newer wording adds kill cord and tender safety equipment.) | DO | Rowing, max persons/load plate, don't overload, painter, lifejackets in the tender, kill cord if outboard. |
| 11 | **Meteorology** | "Awareness of forecasting services and knowledge of the Beaufort scale." | **AWARE** | Beaufort force numbers/descriptions/wind speeds is the only hard-recall item. Plus: where forecasts come from (Shipping Forecast, Inshore Waters, VHF, Navtex, apps). **No synoptic charts, no fronts, no isobars, no sea breeze.** |
| 12 | **Seasickness** | "Working efficiency is unaffected / partially affected / severely affected by seasickness." | **SELF** | Not a knowledge item — it is a three-way tickbox the instructor fills in. Classic Sailing expands it pedagogically ("understanding its potential causes and some remedial measures… being affected by seasickness in no way prevents you from successfully completing the Competent Crew course") but that is their gloss, not logbook text. **At most 2–3 cards, framed as practical mitigation.** |
| 13 | **Helmsmanship and sailing** | "Understands the basic principles of sailing and can steer and trim sails on all points of sailing." / "Can steer a compass course, under sail and power." | DO | Points of sail, no-go zone, luffing/telltales, sheet in/out, weather helm, steering to a compass course, tiller-vs-wheel reversal, "small corrections". |
| 14 | **General duties** | "Has carried out general duties satisfactorily on deck and below decks in connection with the daily routine of the vessel." | DO | Watchkeeping, galley, heads operation, bilge check, stowage, log entries as dictated, fenders and warps on arrival. Mostly un-cardable; a few practical cards (heads rules, gas procedure, fender height). |

### Wording disagreements between sources (real, and they matter)

Two distinct generations of the syllabus text are circulating. Both are reproduced by RYA Recognised Training Centres.

| Item | Legacy wording (G15-era) | Newer wording | Seen on |
|---|---|---|---|
| Section 10 title | "Dinghies" | "**Tender usage**" | Legacy: Hamble, Sailtrain, Nomad, Jolly Parrot, Classic. Newer: Neilson, Endeavour |
| Section 10 content | loading rules + oars | + "Understand the use and importance of the **kill cord**." + "Understand **safety equipment for tenders**." | https://www.neilson.co.uk/sailing/courses/competent-crew-syllabus |
| Section 6 MOB | "action to be taken to recover a man overboard" | + "…and how **cold water shock** can affect a casualty in the water" | Neilson; https://www.endeavour-sailing.co.uk/training-information/rya-practical-syllabi/competent-crew-practical-course |
| Section 8 environment | "responsibility of **yacht skippers** to protect the environment" | "The responsibility of the **boating community** to protect the environment" | Neilson |
| Section 11 met | "Awareness of forecasting services and knowledge of the Beaufort scale" | "Knowledge of the Beaufort Scale. Understands the forecasting services and **where to obtain a suitable weather forecast**." | Neilson |
| Section 5 PSE | "complies with **rules** for the wearing of…" | "complies with the **guidance** for the wearing of…" | Neilson |
| Section 12 | present | present (Neilson, Endeavour, Hamble, Sailtrain, Nomad, Classic) — **absent on Jolly Parrot** | — |

**Recommendation for the deck:** use the **newer** wording where it adds substance (kill cord, cold water shock, "where to obtain a forecast", "boating community"), because it is strictly a superset and reflects the current G158. Keep the legacy section title "Dinghies" only if you want to match older logbooks; "Tender usage" is the safer forward-looking label. Flag in the deck notes that the RYA has not published the full bullet-level syllabus on rya.org.uk, only the 13-item marketing list, so all bullet text is training-centre reproduction.

## 3. Scope boundary vs Day Skipper

### 3.1 The structural difference

| | Competent Crew | Day Skipper Practical |
|---|---|---|
| Role taught | **Crew** — does what the skipper says | **Skipper** — decides |
| Duration | 5 days | 5 days |
| Pre-course experience | **None** | "Basic sailing ability. **5 days, 100 miles and 4 night hours** onboard a sailing yacht." |
| Assumed theory | **None** | "Navigation and theory knowledge to **Day Skipper Shorebased level**" (a separate 40-hour course + exam) |
| Minimum age | Recommended 12 (Sailtrain states 18 as *their own* centre policy) | 16 |
| Written exam | **None** | None on the practical; the Shorebased course has one |
| Night sailing | Not in syllabus (many centres do it anyway) | "Night cruising" is a syllabus section |

Sources: https://www.rya.org.uk/course-finder/competent-crew-practical-course/ ; https://www.rya.org.uk/course-finder/day-skipper-practical-sailing/ ; https://sailtrain.org.uk/competent-crew/ (min age 18 — centre-specific)

RYA's own framing of the boundary, from https://www.rya.org.uk/training/sail-cruising-courses/ :
- Competent Crew: "for beginners and those who would like to become active crew members rather than just passengers. By the end of the course, you should be able to **steer, handle sails, keep a lookout, row a dinghy and assist in all day to day routines**."
- Day Skipper: "the first course in the scheme that focuses on **how to become the skipper**… It aims to teach **pilotage, navigation, seamanship and boat handling** up to the standard required to skipper a sail or motor cruiser safely by day in familiar waters. There is also an introduction to night cruising."

### 3.2 Hard OUT of scope for Competent Crew — zero cards

These appear in the Day Skipper Practical syllabus (https://www.hamble.co.uk/day-skipper-practical-syllabus) and/or Day Skipper Theory (https://www.rya.org.uk/course-finder/day-skipper-theory-course/) and have **no counterpart at all** in the Competent Crew syllabus:

- **Chartwork** — plotting visual fixes, transferred position lines, chart symbols, chart datum
- **DR and EP** — dead reckoning, estimated position, running fix
- **Course to steer** — tidal stream vectors, leeway, drift, variation/deviation, true/magnetic/compass conversion
- **Tides** — tidal height calculation, tidal curves, secondary ports, rule of twelfths, tidal stream atlases, springs/neaps
- **Pilotage** — pilotage plans, leading lines, clearing lines, transits
- **Passage planning** — coastal passage planning, hazards, crew-strength limits
- **IALA buoyage** — lateral/cardinal marks, isolated danger, safe water, special marks
- **Light characteristics** — Fl / Oc / Iso / Q / VQ / LFl, periods, sectored lights, light lists
- **IRPCS in detail** — steering and sailing rules (Rules 5–19), give-way/stand-on, sailing vessel rules (Rule 12), overtaking, narrow channels, restricted visibility, **navigation lights and day shapes**, sound signals
- **Navigation records / logkeeping as navigator**, echo sounder and lead line as navigation instruments
- **Yacht handling under power as skipper** — berthing, springs, mooring buoy pickup, anchoring decisions
- **Engines** — fuel/water filter change, impeller, bleeding the fuel system
- **Maintenance and repair**, rope material properties
- **Victualling**
- **Radiotelephone / VHF distress calls** — Mayday procedure, DSC (this is Day Skipper "Emergency situations"; VHF is a separate SRC course anyway)
- **Towing, helicopter rescue**
- **Night cruising** as a taught competence
- **Preparation for sea as skipper** — engine checks, sail selection, stowage decisions
- **Electronic navigation** — waypoints, chartplotter position fixing
- **Meteorology beyond the basics** — barometer as a forecasting aid, interpreting the Shipping Forecast

### 3.3 Touched at AWARENESS level only — reusable but must be simplified

This is the list that decides whether a Day Skipper card can be reused. Four topics overlap by name and diverge sharply in depth.

**Rules of the road**
- CC bar: *"Is able to keep an efficient lookout at sea."* — one bullet, one skill.
- DS bar: *"Has a working knowledge of the International Regulations for Preventing Collisions at Sea."*
- **Verdict:** almost nothing reusable. A CC "rules of the road" card set should be: what a lookout is for, scanning technique including astern and to leeward behind the genoa, **constant bearing = collision risk**, how to report a sighting to the skipper by clock bearing, and awareness that big ships in a channel cannot manoeuvre. **No give-way/stand-on cards, no light or day-shape cards, no sound-signal cards.** Note that Start Yachting *does* list "Rules of the road" as a content heading (see §5) — so a very light collision-avoidance concept card is defensible, but nothing at Rule-by-Rule depth.

**Meteorology**
- CC bar: *"Awareness of forecasting services and knowledge of the Beaufort scale."* (newer: + "where to obtain a suitable weather forecast")
- DS practical bar: *"Knows sources of forecast information. Can interpret shipping forecasts and use a barometer as a forecasting aid."* DS Theory goes further into synoptics.
- **Verdict:** the **Beaufort scale is the one hard-recall item and it is fully reusable** from a Day Skipper deck. Forecast-source cards reusable if reduced to "name the sources" and stripped of interpretation. **Cut everything on: reading a synoptic chart, warm/cold/occluded fronts, isobar spacing, sea breeze, fog types, and the structure/sequence of a Shipping Forecast bulletin.**

**Man overboard**
- CC bar: *"Understands the action to be taken to recover a man overboard."* — i.e. the crew's immediate actions.
- DS bar: *"Is able to take correct action as skipper for recovery of man overboard"* — the manoeuvre, the decision, the recovery.
- **Verdict:** reuse the *immediate actions* cards verbatim (shout, point, throw, MOB button, keep eyes on). **Do not reuse** cards on the recovery manoeuvre itself (crash stop / quick stop / reach-turn-reach / figure of eight), approach angle, or getting a casualty back aboard as skipper's problem. Cold water shock is in scope under the newer wording.

**Emergency equipment / flares / liferaft**
- CC bar: *"Can operate distress flares and knows when they should be used. Understands how to launch and board a liferaft."*
- DS bar: same content, plus VHF distress, towing, helicopter rescue.
- **Verdict:** flare-type and liferaft cards are **directly reusable** — this is genuinely the same depth in both syllabi. Strip only the VHF/Mayday, tow and helicopter cards.

**Seasickness**
- CC: a tickbox. DS: **identical tickbox**, same three-way wording, appears as DS section 14 on the Hamble DS page.
- **Verdict:** trivially reusable; barely worth cards in either deck.

**Helmsmanship and sailing / General duties**
- CC sections 13 and 14 are **word-for-word identical** to Day Skipper Practical sections 15 and 16 on the Hamble reproductions ("Understands the basic principles of sailing and can steer and trim sails on all points of sailing. Can steer a compass course, under sail and power." / "Has carried out general duties satisfactorily on deck and below decks in connection with the daily routine of the vessel.").
- **Verdict:** **100% reusable, no rewrite needed.** These are shared logbook lines.

**Ropework**
- CC has an explicit named-knot list. DS practical ropework is about warps, springs and *"the properties and uses of common synthetic fibre ropes"* — it assumes the knots.
- **Verdict:** knot cards reused *upward* not downward; a Day Skipper deck's knot cards, if any, are reusable verbatim in CC. Rope-material-property cards are DS-only.

### 3.4 Practical rule for card triage

Ask of each Day Skipper card: **"Would a crew member be given this as an order, or would they have to make this decision?"**
- Given as an order → reusable at CC ("trim the genoa", "tie a bowline in that", "throw the danbuoy").
- Requires a decision, a calculation, a chart, an almanac, a plotter or a tide table → cut.
- Names a rule number, a light character, a buoy shape or a tidal concept → cut.

## 4. Is there a motor-cruising Competent Crew?

**No.** There is no Competent Crew course in the RYA Motor Cruising scheme.

The motor cruising practical ladder, verbatim from https://www.rya.org.uk/training/motor-cruising-courses/ :
1. **Start Motor Cruising** — "a short introduction to motor cruising for novices. By the end of the course you will have experienced steering a motor cruiser, lines and fenders when coming too and leaving a pontoon, ropework and be aware of safety on board. This course is ideal for introducing friends or family members to crewing a motor boat…"
2. **Helmsman** — "This **two-day** course covers basic boat handling, helmsmanship, safety and basic engine maintenance. It aims to teach boat handling & safety within a defined area. It is specific to the type of boat in which the course is carried out… It is useful for **crews as well as skippers**."
3. **Day Skipper Motor** — "This **four-day** course…"
4. **Coastal Skipper Motor**

So the motor-side equivalent of "learn to be useful crew" is split across **Start Motor Cruising** (2-day taster) and **Helmsman** (2 days, boat-specific, handling-focused). Neither maps cleanly onto Competent Crew's 14 sections — Helmsman is a *helming and handling* course including engine basics, not a broad seamanship-and-safety crew course.

**Nuance worth recording:** the official RYA course book *RYA Competent Crew Skills* (product code **CCPCN**, £14.99, 128pp, edition 2014, reprinted 2024, ISBN 9781906435905) is tagged on the RYA shop as the course item for: **"Start Yachting | Cruising Level 1 | Cruising Level 2 | Start Motor Cruising | Competent Crew | Helmsman's Practical"**, and its blurb says *"This edition has been updated to include motor vessels in order to appeal to both sail and power readers."*
Source opened: https://www.rya.org.uk/products/rya-competent-crew-skills/

So the *book* spans sail and power; the *certificate* is sail-only. If you ever want a motor variant of the deck, the source material exists in CCPCN but there is no motor Competent Crew syllabus to map it onto.

## 5. Adjacent entry-level courses — do not pull their content in

All from https://www.rya.org.uk/training/sail-cruising-courses/ unless noted.

**RYA Start Yachting** — 2 days, afloat, no experience, **recommended age 8**.
> "This practical course is a great way to get a feel for sailing and will teach you the fundamentals of how to steer a yacht under power, raise the sails, and handle ropes… By the end of the 2-day course you'll have experienced steering a yacht, sail handling, ropework and be aware of safety on board."
> "Those who complete this RYA Start Yachting certificate can go on to the Competent Crew course and potentially complete it in a reduced time of 3 days or 2 weekends."
Course content: **The yacht / Ropework / Underway / Rules of the road / Man overboard recovery / Clothing and equipment / Emergency equipment and precautions / Meteorology**.
Source opened: https://www.rya.org.uk/training/courses/start-yachting-course-syc
**Risk to you:** Start Yachting is a strict *subset* of Competent Crew, so pulling its content in is harmless-but-redundant. Note it lists "Man overboard **recovery**" and "Clothing and equipment", which are worded differently from CC's "Man overboard" and "Personal safety equipment" — don't create duplicate sections from the naming difference.

**RYA Start Sailing (Cruising Level 1)** — **new-ish, and a genuine scope-confusion risk.**
> "Cruising Level 1 - This course provides a short introduction to sailing for novices. By the end of the course, you will have a basic understanding of Yacht handling under sail including practical techniques and background knowledge. Whilst there is a small element of the syllabus focussed on slipping and coming alongside the primary focus is the sails. Can be run in conjunction with the Basic Skills course."
**Risk:** the name collides exactly with **National Sailing Scheme Level 1 "Start Sailing"** (dinghies, in G4). Different scheme, different boat. If you search for "RYA Start Sailing syllabus" you will get dinghy content. Do not import it.

**RYA Basic Skills (Cruising Level 2)** — 2 days (16 hours), recommended age 12.
> "This course is designed to get you started on your journey to feeling confident manoeuvring a sailing yacht under sail in light winds. It will focus on developing and honing the skills you learnt in level 1 and introducing areas of safety like recovery of a casualty in the water and anchoring. Both the Level 1 and Level 2 courses can be combined."
Pre-course experience: "Basic skills to the level of Sail Cruising Start Sailing or Start Sailing on the National Sailing Scheme." Content: "Rigging, Sailing techniques, Manoeuvring, Ropework."
Source opened: https://www.rya.org.uk/course-finder/cruising-level-2-basic-skills/
**Risk:** name collides with National Sailing Scheme **Level 2 "Basic Skills"** in G4 (dinghies). Also, this is a *helming/handling* course, not a crew course — it includes **anchoring** and **recovery of a casualty in the water**, which are above Competent Crew's bar. Do not import.

**RYA Essential Navigation and Seamanship** — shorebased/online, 16 hours classroom or 8–10 hours online, no experience, no minimum age.
Content: **Charts and Publications / Safety / Engine checks / Buoyage / Tidal awareness / Visual and electronic navigation / Pilotage / Rules of the road / Anchoring / Weather forecasts / Passage planning**.
Source opened: https://www.rya.org.uk/course-finder/essential-navigation-and-seamanship-course/
**Risk: this is the highest-contamination source in the whole set.** It is entry-level *and* it contains buoyage, tides, pilotage, rules of the road and passage planning — every single thing Competent Crew excludes. It is a shorebased theory course, not part of the Competent Crew practical certificate. **Nothing from ENS belongs in a Competent Crew deck**, even though both are marketed as "beginner".

**RYA Day Skipper Theory** — 40 hours plus exam, classroom/online/distance, no minimum age. Content: Seamanship / Coastal navigation and pilotage / Chartwork / Electronic charts / Position fixing / Plotting a course to steer / Weather forecasting and meteorology / Tides / Collision regulations.
Source opened: https://www.rya.org.uk/course-finder/day-skipper-theory-course/
This is where the bulk of your existing Day Skipper deck's chartwork/tides/lights cards come from. **None of it is Competent Crew.**

## 6. What has changed recently — flag list

1. **G15 + G18 → G158 (2015 edition, reprinted 2025).** The cruising syllabus/logbook was consolidated across sail and motor. Almost every training-centre page reproducing the CC syllabus verbatim online is still showing pre-2015 G15 text. https://www.rya.org.uk/products/rya-yachtmaster-scheme-syllabus-and-logbook/
2. **Two live wordings of sections 5, 6, 8, 10, 11** (see §2 table). "Dinghies" → "Tender usage"; kill cord and cold water shock added; "rules" → "guidance" for lifejackets; "yacht skippers" → "boating community". Neilson and Endeavour carry the newer text; Hamble, Sailtrain, Nomad, Jolly Parrot, Classic Sailing carry the older.
3. **New Cruising Level 1 / Level 2 courses in the sail cruising scheme** ("Start Sailing" and "Basic Skills"), sitting alongside Start Yachting and Competent Crew, and reusing names that already exist in the dinghy scheme. The G158 blurb's line "It also introduces new courses" is consistent with this. https://www.rya.org.uk/training/sail-cruising-courses/
4. **The RYA does not publish the bullet-level Competent Crew syllabus on rya.org.uk.** Only the 13-item "Course content" marketing list is online, and it omits Rules of the road and Dinghies/Tender usage. The authoritative bullet text is in the printed G158. Everything at bullet level in this document is therefore third-party reproduction, cross-checked across six centres.
5. **Minimum age is not settled across sources.** RYA says "Recommended age 12"; Sailtrain states 18. Sailtrain's is a centre policy, not the RYA standard. Do not put a minimum age on a card.
6. **"RYA Competent Crew Skills" (CCPCN)** is still the 2014 edition (reprinted 2024) and now covers motor vessels too. If the deck cites a book, cite this one for content and G158 for the syllabus.

## 7. Recommended deck section structure

Fourteen sections, logbook order, newer wording where it is a superset:

1. Sea terms, parts of the boat, rigging and sails
2. Sail handling
3. Ropework
4. Fire precautions and firefighting
5. Personal safety equipment
6. Man overboard
7. Emergency equipment
8. Manners and customs (incl. environment)
9. Rules of the road *(lookout only)*
10. Tender usage *(a.k.a. Dinghies)*
11. Meteorology *(Beaufort + forecast sources only)*
12. Seasickness *(minimal)*
13. Helmsmanship and sailing
14. General duties

Expected card-weight distribution: sections 1, 3, 2 and 13 carry most of the deck; 9, 11 and 12 should be deliberately thin — that thinness *is* the Competent Crew standard, and padding them turns the deck into Day Skipper.
