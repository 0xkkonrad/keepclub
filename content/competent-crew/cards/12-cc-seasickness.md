# 12 Seasickness

## What brings seasickness on, and what makes it worse?

A disagreement inside your own head. Your inner ear feels the boat moving; your eyes, fixed on a cabin that is moving with you, report that nothing is happening. The brain cannot reconcile the two and makes you sick.<br>Everything that widens the gap makes it worse:<br>being <b>below decks</b>, where there is no horizon and the motion is greater the further you are from the middle of the boat;<br>reading, or a phone screen;<br>diesel, cooking and bilge smells;<br>cold and wet;<br>tired, hungry, hungover, or nothing eaten since breakfast.<br>It is not weakness and it happens to people who have sailed for thirty years.

## What actually helps?

Getting out into the air and putting your eyes on the <b>horizon</b>, which is the one thing in sight that is holding still.<br><b>Ask for the helm.</b> Having a job to concentrate on plus something distant to look at is the best cure there is, and it works on people who are already green.<br>Small amounts of plain food, often, and water in sips &mdash; an empty stomach is worse than a full one, and dry biscuits exist for a reason.<br>Warm and dry clothing on before you need it.<br><b>Tablets work taken before you sail</b>, at the interval on the packet. Once you are being sick nothing swallowed stays down long enough to help. Try any remedy ashore first: several of them make you drowsy, and a drowsy lookout is a different problem.<br>And say something early, while somebody can still do something about it.
