# 06 Man overboard

## Someone goes over the side. What do you shout?

<b>'MAN OVERBOARD'</b> &mdash; those words, at the top of your voice, and keep shouting until somebody answers you. Not their name, not 'help'. It is the one phrase everyone on board has been briefed to react to, and it says what has happened without anybody having to ask a question first.

## What is the pointer's job?

One person points at the casualty with a straight arm and <b>does nothing else at all</b> &mdash; not a rope, not a switch, not fetching anything &mdash; until the casualty is back on board. A head in the water is the size of a football and shows for about a second at a time between waves. Glance away to be useful and you will not find it again.

## What goes over the side, and how soon?

Everything that floats, <b>at once</b>: danbuoy, horseshoe lifebuoy, cockpit cushions, a fender. It gives the casualty something to hold and something to be seen by, and it marks roughly where they are. Throw it even if it lands nowhere near them &mdash; it drifts with the water they are in, while the boat is already somewhere else.

## What does the MOB button do, and who presses it?

It drops a mark on the plotter at the position and time it was pressed, so there is a spot to steer back to when there is nothing left to see. Anyone presses it &mdash; whoever is nearest. Press it early, and press it again if you are not certain it took.

## Cold water shock &mdash; what does it do, and why does it kill before hypothermia does?

The first minute is the one that kills. Cold water makes you <b>gasp involuntarily</b> and then breathe fast and hard, and you cannot stop either; if your head is under when you gasp, you inhale water. Remember <b>1-10-1</b>: about <b>one minute</b> of gasping, then roughly <b>ten minutes</b> in which your hands and arms still work well enough to grab a line or a ladder &mdash; use them &mdash; and about <b>an hour</b> before hypothermia takes your consciousness. Being a strong swimmer barely helps.

## You are the one in the water. What do you do?

Get your back to the waves, lean back, put your legs up in front of you, let the lifejacket hold your head clear, and <b>float</b> until the gasping stops &mdash; roughly a minute. Then knees up and arms crossed over your chest, the <b>HELP</b> position, which slows how fast you lose heat. <b>Do not swim after the boat</b>: she is faster than you, she is coming back anyway, and swimming spends the warmth and grip you are going to need.

## The boat is coming back. What is the crew doing?

Still pointing.<br>Boarding ladder down.<br>A line ready with a big bowline already tied in it.<br>Clipped on before anyone goes to the rail.<br>And <b>nobody gets into the water</b>. A rescuer in the water is a second casualty.
