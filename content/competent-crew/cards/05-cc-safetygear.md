# 05 Personal safety equipment

## Lifejacket or buoyancy aid: what is the difference, and what rating do you want for cruising? {ref=safety}

## Is there a law that says you must wear a lifejacket?

On a private boat in the UK, generally no &mdash; what exists is <b>guidance</b>, not a statute. What actually binds you is the <b>skipper's rule</b> on that boat: the skipper says when lifejackets and harnesses go on, and aboard their boat that is not a suggestion. Some countries, and most charter companies, do make it a requirement, so ask before you sail.

## When should the crew wear lifejackets and harnesses? {ref=safety}

## Why does a lifejacket need a crotch strap?

Because the inflated jacket floats and you do not. Without the strap it rides <b>up</b> while you slide <b>down</b> inside it, and you end up wearing it round your ears with your face in the water.

## What do you check on your own lifejacket before you sail?

The gas bottle screwed in <b>hand tight</b>, with no rust and no white powder on it.<br>The firing indicator showing green, not red.<br>The service date.<br>The crotch strap there, and both buckles working.<br>The whistle on its lanyard, and the light &mdash; press it.<br>No tears in the cover.<br>Anything wrong with any of it, say so while you are still alongside.

## Automatic or manual inflation &mdash; which have you got, and why does it matter?

An <b>automatic</b> jacket fires by itself within seconds of hitting the water. A <b>manual</b> one fires only when you pull the toggle. Both have the toggle, and both have an <b>oral tube</b> so you can top it up by mouth. Find out which yours is and where its toggle sits before you leave the pontoon.

## Where may you clip a tether, and where must you never clip it?

Clip on <b>inside the cockpit</b>, before you go out on deck, and stay clipped: to a <b>jackstay</b>, or to a strong point put there for the purpose &mdash; a padeye in the cockpit, a U-bolt at the mast foot.<br>Never to a <b>guardrail</b>, a stanchion, a grab rail or a shroud. Guardrail wire and its fittings are made to stop you leaning off the boat, not to catch a falling adult, and under that load they let go.

## Why does a tether have two hooks?

So that you are never unclipped while you are moving. Clip the free hook on ahead of you, then unclip the one behind &mdash; there is always one on. Where the two legs are different lengths, use the <b>short</b> one on the foredeck, short enough that you cannot get to the water.

## One hand for yourself, one hand for the ship &mdash; what does it mean?

One hand is always holding the boat; the job gets the other one. If it needs both hands, sit down, wedge yourself in, or clip on first. What a lurch takes is your feet, not your grip.

## What should be on your hands and feet on deck?

<b>Feet</b> &mdash; deck shoes or boots with a soft non-marking sole and a real grip pattern. Never bare feet: a cleat or a genoa track breaks toes. Never smooth soles on a wet deck.<br><b>Hands</b> &mdash; sailing gloves whenever you handle loaded rope. A sheet running out through a bare hand takes the skin off it, and then you let go of something you needed to hold.

## Why is staying warm and dry a safety matter rather than a comfort one?

Because cold makes you clumsy, slow and stupid, and it does all three before you notice any of them. Most of the bad calls made on deck belong to someone who got wet three hours earlier. Layers and waterproofs go on <b>before</b> you need them &mdash; once you are wet you will not get warm again at sea &mdash; and wear a hat.
