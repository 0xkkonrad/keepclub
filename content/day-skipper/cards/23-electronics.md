# 23 Electronic navigation

## How does GNSS work, in one sentence?

The receiver measures the time signals from several satellites take to reach it and computes its position from the ranges; four or more satellites give position, altitude and a very accurate time.

## What are the practical limitations of GNSS?

It can be lost or degraded by antenna failure,<br>power failure,<br>jamming or spoofing, and it tells you where you are, not what is around you. Accuracy of a few metres is meaningless if the <b>chart</b> or the datum is less accurate, and the receiver will happily give a position it has interpolated.

## Why can a GNSS position and a paper chart disagree?

Because of the horizontal <b>datum</b>. GNSS works in WGS84; older charts may use a local datum, and the shift can exceed 100 m. Set the receiver to the chart's datum or apply the correction printed on the chart.

## What is a waypoint, and how should you place them?

A position stored in the receiver as a target or a route point. Place them in safe water, not on a buoy or a hazard — never steer straight at a buoy, since everyone else's plotter has it as a waypoint too. Offset them to one side of the mark.

## What are COG and SOG, and how do they differ from heading and log speed?

<b>Course over ground</b> and <b>speed over ground</b> come from the GNSS and include the tide. <b>Heading</b> comes from the compass and <b>log speed</b> from the paddlewheel or ultrasonic log, through the water. Comparing them tells you what the tide is doing.

## What is 'bearing to waypoint' versus 'course to steer'?

The plotter's <b>bearing to waypoint</b> is the direct line from where you are now to the waypoint; it does not allow for tide. A <b>course to steer</b> allows for the tidal stream so you make good the intended track. Steering the bearing to waypoint in a cross-tide sails you round a curve.

## What does an echo sounder actually measure, and what can fool it?

The time for an ultrasonic pulse to return from the bottom. It can be fooled by a shoal of fish,<br>a dense layer of weed or plankton,<br>aeration under the hull from a wave or a turn,<br>and soft mud giving a reading either above or below the true bottom. It also reads from the transducer, not the keel.

## What is a lead line, and why would you use one?

A weighted line marked in metres or fathoms, swung ahead and let touch the bottom. It needs no power,<br>works when the sounder fails,<br>tells you the nature of the bottom if the lead is armed with tallow,<br>and is the right tool for sounding out of a dinghy round an unfamiliar anchorage.

## How does a log measure speed and distance, and what goes wrong?

A paddlewheel, impeller or ultrasonic transducer through the hull, calibrated to read speed and to totalise distance. Paddlewheels foul with weed and barnacles and then under-read badly. Check it against GNSS in still water, and know how to clear it — with the bung ready.

## What can small-craft radar do for you, and what are its limits?

It gives range and bearing of targets and of the coastline in poor visibility, and CPA if it has a plotting function. Limits: a small yacht's scanner is low, so range is short;<br>sea clutter and rain hide small targets;<br>targets in a blind sector behind the mast disappear;<br>and interpreting it takes practice, which is why a radar course is worth doing.

## What is AIS, and what does it not show you?

Ships broadcast identity,<br>position,<br>course,<br>speed and more over VHF; a receiver shows them with CPA and TCPA. It does <b>not</b> show vessels without AIS — most yachts,<br>fishing boats,<br>and anything with it switched off — and it does not show rocks.

## What is a chart plotter's biggest single danger to a navigator?

It shows a small window round the boat symbol, so the wider picture and what lies just off the route disappears. Zoom out regularly, keep a paper chart of the whole passage, and never let the plotter replace thinking about where the dangers are.

## What is NAVTEX?

An automatic text receiver on 518 kHz that prints or displays navigational warnings, weather forecasts and search and rescue information for the area, with no need to be listening at a particular time.

## Why should you cross-check electronic position with an independent method?

Because a single-source position has no error check. Compare the GNSS with a visual fix, a transit, a depth contour or a light bearing. Two independent methods agreeing is a position; one method is an assumption.

## What is the electrical failure plan on a modern yacht?

Know which instruments are on which circuit and where the breakers and fuses are,<br>carry spare fuses,<br>keep the handheld VHF and handheld GNSS charged in the grab bag,<br>keep a paper chart, almanac, plotting instruments, watch and hand-bearing compass at the chart table,<br>and log positions regularly so a DR can be started from the last known one.
