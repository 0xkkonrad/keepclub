# 24 Environment, rules and responsibilities

## What are the skipper's basic legal responsibilities?

The safety of the vessel and everyone aboard;<br>compliance with the IRPCS, local byelaws and the law of the country you are in;<br>keeping the vessel seaworthy and properly equipped;<br>not going to sea in a manner that endangers others;<br>and rendering assistance to anyone in distress at sea.

## What must you do if you receive a distress call?

You are obliged to render assistance to persons in distress at sea if you can do so without serious danger to your own vessel or crew. Acknowledge, report to the Coastguard, and proceed if you can help. Do not assume someone else has answered.

## What may you not discharge overboard?

Oil and oily mixtures, and any garbage other than food waste, are prohibited; plastics are banned absolutely. Sewage discharge is regulated and prohibited in many harbours and marinas — use pump-out facilities where they exist. Chemical toilet contents, oil and old antifouling go ashore.

## What is MARPOL Annex V in practice for a yacht?

Nothing goes over the side except food waste, and only offshore: <b>comminuted</b> food waste, through a 25 mm screen, not less than <b>3 miles</b> from land; uncomminuted not less than <b>12 miles</b>, and 12 miles inside a Special Area. Everything else —<br>plastics,<br>packaging,<br>cans,<br>glass,<br>oily rags,<br>filters,<br>batteries,<br>flares<br>— comes back ashore for disposal at the marina's facilities. Boats of 12 m and over must display a placard of the rules.

## What should you do with out-of-date flares?

Never fire them for fun,<br>never put them in a bin,<br>and never leave them at a police or fire station — that is illegal dumping. <b>HM Coastguard no longer accepts flares for disposal.</b> Try the supplier you bought them from,<br>a liferaft service agent,<br>your harbour or marina,<br>a registered disposal point on The Green Blue's environmental facilities map,<br>a flare amnesty,<br>or a specialist hazardous-waste contractor for a fee. Disposal is the owner's legal responsibility, so check what is available <b>before</b> you buy new ones.

## How should you avoid damaging the seabed when anchoring?

Avoid anchoring on seagrass beds, maerl and other protected habitats, which chain scours badly; use the designated eco-moorings where they exist; and check the chart and local guidance for protected areas before you drop.

## What is your responsibility about wash and speed?

Your wash is your responsibility. Slow down past moorings,<br>dredgers,<br>works,<br>small craft,<br>rowers,<br>swimmers and anyone working on a boat, and observe harbour speed limits — usually 6 knots or less, and often measured over the ground.

## How should you behave around marine wildlife?

Do not chase, encircle or separate animals;<br>keep a steady slow course and let them approach you;<br>avoid seal haul-outs and nesting seabird colonies, especially in the breeding season;<br>and give whales and dolphins a wide, predictable berth.

## What is antifouling and what care does it need?

A coating that stops weed and barnacles growing on the hull. It contains biocides, so scrape and sand it only where there is proper containment ashore, wear protection, and never let residues go into the water.

## What insurance and registration should a cruising yacht have?

Third-party liability insurance at the minimum, and usually full cover; many marinas and foreign authorities require proof. Registration (Part 1 or the Small Ships Register) is needed to prove nationality abroad, and a Ship Radio Licence is required for the VHF.

## What is the ICC and when do you need it?

The International Certificate of Competence, issued in the UK by the RYA, is proof of competence recognised by many countries for their inland and coastal waters. It is not needed in UK waters but is often required or requested abroad, and can be obtained on the strength of a Day Skipper practical certificate.

## What does the RYA Day Skipper practical certificate actually say you can do?

That you have been assessed as able to skipper a 30 to 45 ft cruising yacht and its crew safely <b>by day, on day trips, in familiar waters</b> — doing your own pilotage, navigation, boat handling and passage planning. A certificate gained in non-tidal waters is endorsed as such. It is not a licence, and it is not a Yachtmaster.

## What are the prerequisites for the RYA Day Skipper practical course?

Basic sailing ability plus <b>5 days at sea, 100 miles logged and 4 night hours</b> aboard a sailing yacht, and navigation and theory to <b>Day Skipper shorebased standard</b> — the shorebased certificate itself is not compulsory. Minimum age <b>16</b>. The course runs as 5 days,<br>3 weekends,<br>or 3 days plus 2.

## What does the Day Skipper shorebased course cover and how long is it?

About 40 hours plus exam time:<br>seamanship,<br>coastal navigation and pilotage,<br>chartwork,<br>electronic charts,<br>position fixing,<br>plotting a course to steer,<br>weather and meteorology,<br>tides,<br>and the collision regulations. It is assessed by written papers, with chart 5011, an exercise chart and an almanac extract to hand.
