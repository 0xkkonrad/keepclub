# 18 Restricted visibility

## What actions do you take when fog closes in?

Fix your position immediately while you still can.<br>Slow to a safe speed, engine on and ready.<br>Sound the fog signal.<br>Show navigation lights.<br>Lifejackets on and crew on deck as extra lookouts, listening as well as looking.<br>Hoist the radar reflector.<br>Get out of shipping lanes and busy channels.<br>Plot continuously and consider anchoring in shallow water clear of the fairway.

## Which rule governs your conduct in fog, and what is different about it?

<b>Rule 19</b>. It applies when vessels are not in sight of one another in or near restricted visibility, and there is <b>no stand-on vessel</b> — every vessel has an obligation to avoid collision.

## Why is it safer to be in shallow water in fog?

Because commercial traffic cannot follow you there. Getting outside the buoyed channel into water too shallow for shipping, or anchoring there, removes the biggest single threat.

## Why anchor rather than keep moving in thick fog?

An anchored boat is not closing anything,<br>is easier to fix,<br>sounds a distinctive signal,<br>and lets you listen properly. Anchor clear of the channel,<br>sound the bell signal,<br>and keep a lookout.

## What should you listen for in fog, and how reliable is it?

Fog signals,<br>engine noise,<br>breaking water on a shore,<br>and bell buoys. Sound in fog is deceptive: it can be deflected, reflected and blanketed, so a signal can seem to come from the wrong direction or be inaudible in a 'shadow'. Never bet your position on hearing alone.

## How useful is a small-craft radar reflector in practice?

Better than nothing, but a yacht is a poor radar target, especially heeled or in a rough sea where sea clutter hides her. Assume you have <b>not</b> been seen and keep out of the way.

## What is AIS and how does it help in poor visibility?

The Automatic Identification System broadcasts a vessel's identity, position, course and speed over VHF. A receiver shows you shipping with its CPA and TCPA. An AIS transponder makes you visible to them too. It does not show vessels without AIS, and small craft often carry none.

## What is CPA and TCPA?

<b>Closest Point of Approach</b> — how near the other vessel will pass if nothing changes.<br><b>Time to CPA</b> — how long until that happens. They are the two numbers that tell you whether you have a problem and how long you have to solve it.

## What are the fog signals you are most likely to hear from a big ship, and what do they mean?

One prolonged blast at intervals of <b>not more than</b> 2 minutes — a power-driven vessel <b>making way</b>.<br>Two prolonged blasts about 2 seconds apart — a power-driven vessel underway but <b>stopped</b>.<br>One prolonged and two short — a sailing vessel, or a vessel fishing, towing or pushing, not under command, restricted in her ability to manoeuvre, or constrained by draught. All at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.

## Should you use the engine in fog if you are under sail?

Usually yes: it gives you manoeuvrability and the ability to stop, which Rule 19 expects. But turn it off periodically, or have a crew member well forward, so you can listen. Remember that once under power you must show the correct lights, hoist the black <b>cone, point down</b>, forward by day, and that your fog signal changes from one prolonged and two short to <b>one prolonged</b>.

## What is the greatest danger to a yacht in fog in the Channel?

Being run down by shipping that cannot see you and may not detect you on radar, especially in or near a traffic separation scheme or the approaches to a major port. Cross the lanes at right angles, quickly, and preferably not in fog at all.
