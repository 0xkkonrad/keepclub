# 21 Boat handling and deck work

## What are the two things that always determine how you approach a berth, buoy or anchorage?

The <b>wind</b> and the <b>tide</b>, and which of the two is stronger. Approach into the stronger one so it slows you down and you keep steerage way at low speed over the ground.

## How do you come alongside a pontoon under power?

Assess wind and tide, and approach into the stronger of them at a shallow angle, at the slowest speed that keeps steerage. Rig fenders and lines both sides in advance,<br>brief the crew,<br>stop the boat with a burst astern,<br>and get a midships or bow line ashore first — then springs and the rest.

## Which line do you get ashore first when coming alongside, and why?

A <b>midships line</b>, taken to a cleat abreast of or just ahead of the boat, so it both checks the boat's way and holds her in — one line and one crew member can hold a yacht alongside while you sort out the rest. Bow and stern lines and springs follow. Never send a crew member jumping ashore with a line: step off, do not leap.

## How do you leave a pontoon berth with the wind pinning you on?

Decide which end you want out.<br><b>Stern out:</b> keep a bow line and a bow spring — bow led to a cleat aft of you — and motor gently <b>ahead</b> against it with the helm towards the pontoon; the stern swings out, then go astern out of the berth.<br><b>Bow out:</b> keep a stern line and a stern spring — stern led to a cleat ahead of you — and motor gently <b>astern</b> against it with the helm towards the pontoon; the bow swings out, then drive ahead. Fender the pivot point and use slipped lines so nobody is left ashore.

![diagram](fig:mooring-lines@back-spring,fenders)

## What is a slipped line, and why use one?

A line taken round a cleat or ring ashore and back to the boat, so the crew can release it from on board without anyone going ashore. It is how you leave a berth or a buoy short-handed.

## How do you pick up a mooring buoy?

Work out which way the boats already on the moorings are lying, approach from downwind or down-tide into the stronger of the two, aim to arrive with just enough way to stop with the buoy at the bow, and have a crew member with a boat hook and a line rigged through the bow fairlead ready to pass through the buoy's ring or pick up the pick-up buoy.

## How do you handle a boat going astern?

Slowly, and expect prop walk to pull the stern one way. There is no prop wash over the rudder, so you need way on before the rudder does anything. Look astern,<br>put the helm over gently,<br>and use short bursts of power rather than continuous revs.

![diagram](fig:prop-walk@astern)

## How do you turn a yacht in a confined space under power?

Use a <b>standing turn</b>: helm hard over in the direction you want to turn, and <b>leave it there</b>. A short burst ahead sends prop wash over the rudder and kicks the stern round, swinging the bow. A short burst astern kills the headway — accepting that the rudder does very little astern and prop walk will dominate. Alternate short bursts and the boat pivots with almost no forward movement. Turn the way prop walk helps you, not against it.

![diagram](fig:standing-turn)

## Why should you never use your hands or feet to fend off?

Because a few tonnes of boat crushes fingers and legs. Use a fender or a boat hook, and if the boat is going to hit, let it hit — the gel coat is cheaper than a hand.

## How do you rig a headsail correctly?

Hank on or feed the luff into the foil from the tack up,<br>shackle the tack,<br>attach the halyard to the head making sure it is not twisted round the forestay,<br>lead both sheets outside the shrouds through the fairleads to the winches,<br>and put stopper knots in the ends.

## What is sail trim on the different points of sailing?

Close hauled — sails hard in. Close reach — eased slightly. Beam reach — about half out. Broad reach — well out. Run — right out, boom well off and the headsail goosewinged or poled out. The rule is to ease until the luff just lifts, then pull in until it stops.

## What are the signs you are over-canvassed and should reef?

Excessive heel,<br>heavy weather helm,<br>the boat slowing and slamming rather than accelerating,<br>the leeward rail down,<br>the crew uncomfortable,<br>and the helm working hard to keep her on track. If you are wondering whether to reef, reef.

## How do you put a reef in with slab reefing?

Head up or heave to,<br>take up on the topping lift,<br>ease the kicker and the mainsheet,<br>drop the halyard to the mark,<br>hook or pull down the luff cringle at the tack,<br>tension the halyard,<br>haul in the leech reefing line to the clew,<br>tension the foot,<br>ease the topping lift and re-set the kicker,<br>then bear away and trim.

## How do you shake out a reef?

In the reverse order:<br>take up the topping lift,<br>ease the halyard slightly and the reefing line,<br>free the luff cringle,<br>then hoist the halyard fully,<br>re-tension the outhaul and kicker,<br>ease the topping lift,<br>and trim. Make sure the reefing line runs free so it does not tear the sail.

## How do you heave to, and why would you?

Tack the boat without releasing the headsail sheet so the headsail is backed, then lash the helm to leeward and ease the mainsail. The boat lies quietly at a slow drift. Use it to reef,<br>eat,<br>sort out a problem,<br>wait for a tide or a lock,<br>or make a repair.

## What is weather helm and lee helm?

<b>Weather helm</b> — the boat tries to turn up into the wind, so you hold the <b>tiller to windward</b> (wheel to leeward) to keep her straight; that is where the name comes from. A little is desirable; a lot means too much mainsail or too much heel.<br><b>Lee helm</b> — she tries to turn away from the wind and you hold the tiller to leeward; it is dangerous, and usually means too much headsail or too little main.

## How does a yacht behave differently under sail when you berth or anchor?

You cannot stop instantly and you cannot go astern, so you must plan an approach that lets you spill wind from the sails to control speed — head-to-wind or close-reaching in — with an escape route if it goes wrong. Have the anchor ready, and know where you will bear away to if you have to abort.

## What are the commands for tacking and gybing?

Tacking: 'Ready about?' — crew confirm — 'Lee-oh' as you put the helm down.<br>Gybing: 'Stand by to gybe' — check the mainsheet and kicker are in and the boom controlled — 'Gybe-oh'. In both cases the point is that everyone knows what is about to happen.

## What is an accidental gybe, and how do you avoid one?

The boom crashing across uncontrolled when running or on a broad reach — it can knock someone overboard or break the rig. Avoid it by not sailing dead downwind in a seaway,<br>rigging a preventer,<br>watching the wind indicator and the helm,<br>and briefing the crew to keep their heads down when running.

## What is a preventer and when do you rig it?

A line from the end of the boom led forward to a strong point on the foredeck to stop the boom crossing in an accidental gybe. Rig it whenever you are running or on a broad reach, especially at night or in a swell.

## How do you handle a yacht in strong wind under power?

Reduce speed to keep the bow from slamming,<br>steer to take the seas at a slight angle rather than dead on,<br>expect the bow to blow off downwind at low speed,<br>allow much more room for every manoeuvre,<br>and remember you may not be able to hold a head-to-wind position at all.

## What is the effect of waves on boat handling and crew?

They slow the boat,<br>throw her off course,<br>make the deck dangerous<br>and cause seasickness and fatigue. Slowing down and altering course a few degrees to change the angle of encounter often transforms the ride, and a tired crew makes mistakes — plan watches and hot food.

## How do you rig for a lock?

Fenders both sides, long bow and stern lines ready to loop over the bollards or a ladder rung, and one crew on each. Keep the lines slipped from on board and tend them as the water level changes — never make fast solid in a rising or falling lock.

## How do you go bow-to or stern-to a quay with a lazy line, Mediterranean style?

Approach slowly straight in,<br>drop the anchor or pick up the lazy line at the marked point,<br>get two stern lines ashore to the quay bollards,<br>then tension the lazy line or anchor cable to hold the bow off. Have a boarding plank or ladder ready, and fenders where the neighbours are.

## How do you raft up alongside another yacht?

Ask permission on approach.<br>Rig fenders high and at the widest points, and approach into the stronger of wind and tide as you would a pontoon.<br>Get your bow and stern lines onto the inside boat, then run <b>your own shore lines</b> — a bow line and a stern line to the pontoon — so your weight is not hanging on your neighbour.<br>Cross their <b>foredeck</b>, never the cockpit, and stagger the masts so the rigs cannot clash in a swell.<br>Agree who is leaving when.

## How do you come alongside when a tidal stream is running along the berth?

Always approach <b>against</b> the stream, even if that means passing the berth and turning to come back. Head up into it at a shallow angle at the speed that gives you steerage over the ground — which can be several knots through the water while barely moving over it — and use the stream as your brake. Get the midships or upstream line ashore first. Coming alongside downstream you cannot stop, and the stream will take the bow out.

## Preparing for sea: name five things you do below decks and five on deck.

<b>Below:</b> stow everything so nothing can fly across in a knockdown,<br>lockers and floorboards secured,<br>heavy gear low and central,<br>washboards and hatch to hand,<br>gas off at the bottle,<br>kettle and flask filled,<br>seacocks checked,<br>bilge dry,<br>chart and plan at the chart table.<br><b>On deck:</b> sail ties and halyards clear and led,<br>sheets rove and stoppered,<br>anchor secured,<br>warps and fenders stowed and not trailing,<br>dinghy and outboard lashed,<br>jackstays rigged,<br>lifebuoy and danbuoy free to release,<br>tender painter clear of the propeller. Then walk round and look at it all again.
