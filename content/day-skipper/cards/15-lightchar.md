# 15 Light characteristics

## What does F mean on a chart?

Fixed — a continuous, steady light that does not change.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does Fl mean, and what is the pattern?

Flashing — a single flash at regular intervals, where the duration of <b>light is shorter</b> than the duration of dark. Fl 5s = one flash every 5 seconds.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does Fl(3) 15s mean?

Group flashing — a group of <b>three</b> flashes, the whole pattern repeating every <b>15 seconds</b>.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does LFl mean?

Long flashing — a single flash of <b>2 seconds or more</b>, repeated. LFl 10s = one long flash every 10 seconds.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does Oc mean, and how do you recognise it?

Occulting — the <b>light is longer than the dark</b>: a steady light briefly eclipsed. Oc(2) 7s = two eclipses in a 7-second period.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does Iso mean?

Isophase — the periods of light and dark are <b>exactly equal</b>.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What do Q and VQ mean?

<b>Q</b> quick flashing, 50-79 flashes per minute.<br><b>VQ</b> very quick flashing, 80-159 per minute. (UQ, ultra quick, is 160 or more.)

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does Mo(A) mean, and where would you see it?

Morse code letter A — a short flash followed by a long flash. Commonly used on <b>safe water marks</b> and landfall buoys.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does Al mean?

Alternating — the light shows different colours in succession from the same position, e.g. Al WR.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What is the 'period' of a light?

The time taken for one complete cycle of the light's pattern, from the start of one group to the start of the next. It is the number of seconds printed after the rhythm, and it is what you actually time with a watch to identify a light.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## Decode: Fl(2)WRG 10s 15m 11-8M.

Two flashes every 10 seconds;<br>the light shows <b>white, red and green sectors</b>;<br>its elevation is <b>15 m above the chart height datum</b>, which is MHWS on UK charts;<br>and the nominal ranges are <b>white 11 M, green 8 M, red between the two</b> — the first figure is always white and the last green.

## Decode: Oc(2)R 7s 9m 6M.

An occulting red light, two eclipses in a 7-second period, 9 metres above MHWS, nominal range 6 miles.

## Decode: Q(9) 15s.

Nine quick flashes every 15 seconds — a <b>West cardinal</b> mark.

![diagram](ds-cardinal-marks.png)

## Decode: Fl(2+1) R 10s.

A group of two flashes then a single flash, red, every 10 seconds — a <b>preferred channel to starboard</b> mark in Region A.

![diagram](ds-iala-lateral.png)

## If a light shows no colour letter, what colour is it?

White. Colours are lettered only when they are not white: R red,<br>G green,<br>Y yellow,<br>Bu blue,<br>Vi violet,<br>Or orange.

## How do you positively identify a light at night?

Time the full period with a watch, count the flashes in a group, note the colour, and compare all three with the chart or light list. Then check the bearing is consistent with your expected position. Never identify a light on colour alone.

## What is a lighthouse's 'racon' marking on the chart?

A radar transponder beacon: it paints a Morse identification letter on a radar display outward from its position, giving a range and bearing. Charted as 'Racon(T)' or similar.

## What does IQ mean?

Interrupted quick flashing — continuous quick flashing with a regular eclipse. IVQ and IUQ are the very quick and ultra quick equivalents.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## What does FFl mean?

Fixed and flashing — a steady light with a brighter flash superimposed at regular intervals.

![diagram](ds-light-characteristics.png)

## On an Admiralty chart, what is a light's elevation measured from, and what does its charted range mean?

Elevation is the height of the <b>focal plane above the chart's height datum</b> — MHWS on UK charts — not above the sea level at the time.<br>Charted ranges are <b>nominal ranges</b> in sea miles: the luminous range in a meteorological visibility of 10 sea miles.

## A light is charted 'Fl WRG 5s 12m 11-8M'. What is the range of each colour?

<b>White 11 M, green 8 M, red between the two.</b> The first figure is always white and the last is green; intermediate colours lie between.

## For a sector light or a leading line, which way are the charted bearings measured?

<b>Always from seaward</b>, towards the light. To use one as a course to steer inbound you read it straight off the chart; to leave on it you use the reciprocal.
