# 10 Compass, variation, deviation and leeway

## What is variation?

The angle between <b>true</b> north and <b>magnetic</b> north at a particular place and date. It is read from the chart's compass rose and changes slowly year on year.

![diagram](ds-variation-deviation.png)

## What is deviation?

The error caused by the <b>boat's own</b> magnetic field acting on the compass. It changes with the boat's heading, so it is tabulated on a deviation card, and it can be changed by moving anything magnetic near the compass.

![diagram](ds-variation-deviation.png)

## Give the rule for converting between true, magnetic and compass.

<b>Error WEST, compass BEST</b> — a westerly error makes the compass number bigger, so going true to compass you <b>add</b> it.<br><b>Error EAST, compass LEAST</b> — going true to compass you <b>subtract</b> an easterly error. Reverse both when going from compass to true.

![diagram](ds-variation-deviation.png)

## Chart course 145&deg;T, variation 3&deg;W, deviation 2&deg;E. What do you steer?

145 + 3 (variation west) = 148&deg;M. 148 &minus; 2 (deviation east) = <b>146&deg;C</b>.

![diagram](ds-variation-deviation.png)

## Compass heading 210&deg;C, deviation 4&deg;W, variation 5&deg;W. What is the true heading?

Going compass to true you subtract westerly errors: 210 &minus; 4 = 206&deg;M, 206 &minus; 5 = <b>201&deg;T</b>.

## How do you check the deviation of a steering compass without a swing?

Compare it against a known transit: line up two charted objects, read the compass, and compare with the transit's charted bearing after applying variation. Do it on several headings. Also compare the steering compass with a hand-bearing compass held clear of metal.

## What causes deviation to change unexpectedly?

Anything ferrous or magnetic moved near the compass — a handheld VHF,<br>a mobile phone,<br>a laptop,<br>tinned food,<br>a torch,<br>tools,<br>an outboard,<br>or a loudspeaker. Keep the area around the compass clear and check it if the reading looks odd.

## Where should you stand to take a hand-bearing compass reading?

Somewhere with a clear all-round view and well away from the engine, rigging, winches, electronics and any iron — often the after end of the cockpit or the coachroof. Treat a hand-bearing compass as having no deviation only after you have checked it in that spot.

## What is leeway and how do you allow for it?

The sideways slip of the boat through the water caused by the wind, so the track lies <b>downwind</b> of the heading. Estimate it from your own boat — typically 5-10&deg; hard on the wind, less as you bear away, none dead downwind or motoring in calm water. To make good the track you want, steer <b>upwind</b> of it by the leeway angle.

![diagram](ds-variation-deviation.png)

## How can you measure your own leeway?

Look astern at the wake: the angle between the wake and the fore-and-aft line is the leeway. Or compare your compass heading with the ground track from the GNSS after allowing for tide.

## In what order do you apply leeway, variation and deviation?

Work the plot in <b>true</b>. Apply <b>leeway</b> to convert between the water track and the heading, then convert that true heading to magnetic with <b>variation</b>, then to compass with <b>deviation</b>.

## What is a fluxgate compass and what feeds off it?

An electronic compass that senses the earth's field with no moving card. It feeds the autopilot, the instrument repeaters and often the radar and plotter heading display. It still has deviation and needs calibrating by swinging the boat in a circle.

## Why keep a magnetic steering compass when the plotter shows heading?

Because it needs no power. In an electrical failure the magnetic compass, the log, the chart, the almanac and a watch are what get you home.

## Variation is 4&deg;W in 2020 with annual change 8'E. What is it in 2026?

Six years at 8' east per year is 48' east, so 4&deg;00'W &minus; 48' = about <b>3&deg;12'W</b>. In practice round to the nearest degree for chartwork: 3&deg;W.

## Why must a plotted bearing taken with a hand-bearing compass be converted before you draw it?

Because the compass reads magnetic and the chart is drawn true. Apply variation (and any deviation) to get a true bearing, then plot it from the object towards your position.
