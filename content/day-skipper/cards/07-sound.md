# 07 Sound signals

## Define a short blast and a prolonged blast.

A <b>short blast</b> is about 1 second. A <b>prolonged blast</b> is 4 to 6 seconds.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## What do one, two and three short blasts mean?

Given by a <b>power-driven vessel underway</b>, when <b>in sight</b> of another vessel and actually manoeuvring: <b>one</b> — 'I am altering my course to starboard';<br><b>two</b> — 'I am altering my course to port';<br><b>three</b> — 'I am operating astern propulsion'. They are statements of what you <b>are doing</b>, not requests — and they are not fog signals.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## What do five or more short and rapid blasts mean?

'I do not understand your intentions' or 'I doubt whether you are taking sufficient action to avoid collision' (Rule 34(d)). It is the wake-up signal.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## In a narrow channel, what signal means 'I intend to overtake you on your starboard side'?

Two prolonged blasts followed by one short blast. For the port side: two prolonged followed by two short. The vessel to be overtaken agrees with one prolonged, one short, one prolonged, one short.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## What signal do you sound when approaching a bend where other vessels may be hidden?

One <b>prolonged</b> blast (Rule 34(e)). Any vessel within earshot round the bend answers with one prolonged blast.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Fog signal — power-driven vessel making way through the water

One <b>prolonged</b> blast at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Fog signal — power-driven vessel underway but stopped and making no way

<b>Two prolonged</b> blasts in succession, about 2 seconds apart, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Fog signal — sailing vessel underway

One prolonged followed by <b>two short</b> blasts, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes. The same signal is used by a vessel not under command,<br>restricted in her ability to manoeuvre,<br>constrained by draught,<br>fishing,<br>or towing or pushing.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Fog signal — vessel at anchor

Rapid ringing of the bell for about 5 seconds at intervals of not more than one minute. In a vessel of <b>100 m or more</b>, the bell forward and, immediately after, the gong sounded rapidly for about 5 seconds aft. She may also sound one short, one prolonged and one short blast to warn an approaching vessel of her position.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Fog signal — vessel aground

Three separate and distinct strokes on the bell, then the rapid ringing, then three more strokes, at intervals of not more than one minute — plus, if appropriate, a whistle signal.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Fog signal — vessel being towed

The vessel towed, or the last vessel of the tow if manned, sounds one prolonged followed by <b>three short</b> blasts at intervals of not more than 2 minutes, and where practicable immediately after the towing vessel's signal.

## What are the sound signalling requirements for a vessel of less than 12 m?

She is not obliged to carry the prescribed bell or whistle, but she <b>must</b> carry some other means of making an efficient sound signal, and must use it at intervals of not more than 2 minutes.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Fog signal — pilot vessel on duty

In addition to her other signals she may sound an identity signal of <b>four short blasts</b>.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## Rule 36 — what may you do to attract attention?

Make any light or sound signal that cannot be mistaken for one of the prescribed signals, or direct a searchlight beam towards the danger, in a way that does not embarrass any other vessel. A high-intensity intermittent or revolving light such as a strobe is not to be used.

## What sound signal accompanies a distress situation?

Continuous sounding of any fog-signalling apparatus is a recognised distress signal (Annex IV). So is a gun or other explosive signal fired at intervals of about a minute.

## What sound signalling equipment must a vessel carry?

Rule 33. <b>12 m or more</b> — a whistle <b>and</b> a bell.<br><b>100 m or more</b> — a gong in addition, of a tone that cannot be confused with the bell.<br><b>Under 12 m</b> — none of these is required, but she must have some other means of making an efficient sound signal, used at intervals of not more than 2 minutes in fog.

![diagram](ds-sound-signals.png)

## May manoeuvring signals be made with a light?

Yes (Rule 34(b)). Any vessel may supplement the whistle signals with an all-round white light: <b>one flash</b> altering to starboard,<br><b>two</b> to port,<br><b>three</b> operating astern propulsion, repeated while the manoeuvre is carried out. Each flash about a second, about a second between flashes, at least 10 seconds between successive signals.
