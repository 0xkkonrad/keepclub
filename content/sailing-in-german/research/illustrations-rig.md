The sail caption describes the existing triangular mainsail and its orientation. [Sailnator's illustrated terminology](https://sailnator.de/begriffe/) supports Großsegel, Mast, Baum, Bug, and the sail anatomy. The caption's statement that the bow lies to the right comes from the source illustration; it is not a general rule about sails.

The rig caption uses Stehendes Gut for mast-supporting stays and shrouds, consistent with [Segelcrew's sailing lexicon](https://segelcrew.at/lexikon/s). Laufendes Gut is used for rigging that sets and trims sails, as defined in [boats2sail's sailing-school lexicon](https://boats2sail.com/lexikon/laufendes-gut/) and [Rolf Dreyer's sailing-school terminology](https://www.sportbootfuehrerschein.de/wissen/laufendes-gut.html). This also grounds both colored SVG legends.

## Translation choices

- The lower forward corner is Hals. Wende would name a manoeuvre.
- The upper corner is Segelkopf, matching the existing card. The sources use the shorter Kopf for the same corner.
- The rear stay is Achterstag. [Achterstag](https://www.segeln-lernen.de/segellexikon-achterstag.html) and [Backstag](https://www.segeln-lernen.de/segellexikon-backstag.html) are distinct rigging arrangements.
- The boom-supporting line is Dirk, and the tackle holding the boom down is Baumniederholer. These functions are distinct in the drawing and in the [Dirk](https://www.segeln-lernen.de/segellexikon-dirk.html) and [Baumniederholer](https://www.segeln-lernen.de/segellexikon-baumniederholer.html) entries.
- Wanten retains the plural. Salinge retains the plural spreaders, attested by [Mytilus's sailing lexicon](https://www.mytilus.de/segellexikon.html).
