# 16 Home and places

## What does "tomo" mean?

House, building, or room &mdash; any enclosed, indoor structure. <b>tomo moku</b> ("eating house") is one common way to build the idea of "restaurant" out of pieces.

## What does "supa" mean?

A flat, raised surface meant to be used &mdash; a table, chair, bed, shelf, or similar piece of furniture. What unites them is the flat surface something rests or sits on.

## What does "poki" mean?

Container &mdash; box, cup, bag, bowl, or anything that holds something else. <b>poki telo</b> is "water container," a cup or bottle depending on size.

## What does "esun" mean?

Market, shop, trade, or a business transaction &mdash; both the place and the act of exchanging goods. <b>mi tawa esun</b> is "I'm going to the market/store."

## What does "tenpo" mean?

Time, a moment, or a period/duration &mdash; the general word for anything to do with when or how long. <b>tenpo pini</b> is "past time" (the past); <b>tenpo kama</b> is "coming time" (the future).

## What does "ijo" mean?

Thing, something, or stuff &mdash; the most general placeholder noun for an object whose exact nature doesn't matter or isn't known. <b>ijo li lon poki</b> is "there's something in the container."

## What does "nasin" mean?

Way, path, road, method, or system &mdash; a route through space or a route through doing something. <b>nasin tawa</b> is literally "a way of going," a road, but also loosely "a way of doing things," a method or custom.

## Translate into Toki Pona: "The thing is at the market."

<b>ijo li lon esun.</b>
