# 07 Questions and commands

## What does "seme" do, and where does it go in a sentence?

It's the question word for "what" or "which," and it sits exactly where the answer would go, rather than moving to the front the way English "what" does. <b>sina wile e seme?</b> is "you want [what]?" &mdash; "what do you want?" <b>ni li seme?</b> is "this is [what]?" &mdash; "what is this?"

## How do you ask a yes/no question in Toki Pona?

By repeating the word being asked about with <b>ala</b> in between: <b>sina moku ala moku?</b>, literally "you eat not-eat?" &mdash; "do you eat?" The answer is usually just the word alone (<b>moku</b> for yes) or with <b>ala</b> (<b>moku ala</b>, or plain <b>ala</b>, for no).

## What does the particle "o" do in a command?

It replaces <b>li</b> before the action, turning a statement into an instruction or suggestion aimed at the subject. <b>sina o moku</b> is "you should eat"; <b>mi mute o tawa</b> is "let's go" (we should go) &mdash; the subject can be "you," "we," or a third party being told what to do.

## How do you address someone directly using "o"?

By placing <b>o</b> right after their name or description, as a stand-alone vocative &mdash; the "hey, [name]" of Toki Pona. <b>jan Ana o, sina pona</b> is "Jan Ana, you're good/kind," calling her attention before the sentence continues.

## What does adding "anu seme" to the end of a sentence do?

It turns a statement into a tag question, literally "or what?" <b>sina wile e ni, anu seme?</b> is "you want this, or what?" &mdash; close to English "...right?"

## What does "ni" mean, and how is it used?

"This" or "that," Toki Pona's one general demonstrative &mdash; it doesn't distinguish near from far the way English does. Alone it acts as a pronoun: <b>ni li pona</b>, "this is good." After a noun it acts as a modifier: <b>tomo ni li suli</b>, "this house is big."
