# 01 What Toki Pona is

## What is Toki Pona?

A constructed language built around a small core vocabulary and a simple, regular grammar. Instead of naming things precisely, it names broad concepts and leaves you to combine and narrow them with context &mdash; a deliberate constraint that shapes how you think about what you're saying, not just how you say it.

## What does the name "toki pona" mean?

Literally <b>good language</b> or <b>simple language</b> &mdash; <b>toki</b> means language/speech/to talk, and <b>pona</b> means good/simple. The name is also a small demonstration: it's built entirely from words the language itself teaches you in this course.

## Who created Toki Pona, and when?

The Canadian linguist and translator Sonja Lang, who published its core vocabulary online in 2001 and spent the following thirteen years refining it with the community that grew up around it &mdash; work that culminated in the 2014 book <i>Toki Pona: The Language of Good</i>. The whole project began as a personal philosophical question she set out to answer: how few concepts can a language get away with while staying genuinely usable for daily life?

## Why does Toki Pona use so few words?

Its core vocabulary is about 120 words &mdash; tiny next to a natural language's tens of thousands. The deliberate constraint encourages speakers to break ideas into broad, relevant qualities and rely on context instead of reaching for a separate word for every distinction.

## If the vocabulary is so small, how do you say specific things?

By combining words. There's no word for "car," but <b>tomo tawa</b> &mdash; "moving container/building" &mdash; gets there, and context narrows it further. The trade is precision for effort: you say less exactly, but you say it fast, and the listener fills in the rest from context, the same way you'd understand "the vehicle" from context without needing "vehicle" to specify make and model.

## Is Toki Pona meant to replace other languages?

No. People generally learn it as an additional language, for the community around it, as a thinking exercise, for art, or simply for the fun of a genuinely different design. It is not tied to a country and was not designed to become a universal language.

## Does Toki Pona have its own writing system?

The language itself is usually written with the Latin alphabet, exactly as it's taught here. A separate optional script, <b>sitelen pona</b>, represents each word as a small pictogram instead of letters &mdash; popular in the community for art and signage, but not needed to read, speak, or study the language.

## Build a Toki Pona phrase for "car" from "tomo" (building/container) and "tawa" (moving).

<b>tomo tawa</b> &mdash; literally "moving building/container."
