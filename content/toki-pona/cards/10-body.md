# 10 The body

## What does "sijelo" mean?

Body &mdash; the whole physical form of a person or animal, as opposed to its mind or its individual parts. <b>sijelo mi</b> is "my body."

## What does "lawa" mean?

Head, literally &mdash; and by extension, to lead, control, or rule, and the mind or leader doing the controlling. <b>lawa mi</b> is "my head," while <b>jan lawa</b> is "the leader," the person who "heads" a group.

## What does "noka" mean?

Leg or foot, and by extension the bottom or base of something &mdash; the part that carries the rest. <b>noka mi</b> is "my leg/foot"; <b>noka tomo</b> could describe a building's foundation.

## What does "uta" mean?

Mouth, and by extension a doorway or opening something eats or speaks through. Most directly, it's the body part: <b>uta mi</b>, "my mouth."

## What does "insa" mean?

Inside, interior, or stomach/belly &mdash; both the general idea of "the inside of something" and the specific body part where that's most obviously felt. <b>insa tomo</b> is "inside the house"; <b>insa mi li ike</b> is "my stomach feels bad."

## What does "monsi" mean?

Back (the body part) or behind &mdash; both the physical back and the position "behind" something. <b>monsi mi</b> is "my back"; <b>monsi tomo</b> is "behind the house."

## What does "sinpin" mean?

Face, front, or wall &mdash; the front-facing surface of a person, a place, or a structure. <b>sinpin mi</b> is "my face"; <b>sinpin tomo</b> is "the front of the house" or "a wall."

## What does "selo" mean?

Skin, peel, shell, or bark &mdash; and more generally, the outer layer or boundary of anything. <b>selo mi</b> is "my skin"; <b>selo kili</b> is a fruit's peel.
