# 17 Everyday actions

## What does "pali" mean?

To work, make, do, or act &mdash; and, as a noun, work or a job. <b>mi pali e tomo</b> is "I make/build a house"; <b>mi pali</b> alone is simply "I work" or "I'm doing something."

## What does "pana" mean?

To give, put, send, release, or produce &mdash; moving something from one place, person, or state to another. <b>mi pana e ni tawa sina</b> is "I give this to you."

## What does "jo" mean?

To have, hold, or contain &mdash; possession or physical containment, treated as the same idea. <b>mi jo e ilo</b> is "I have a tool."

## What does "moku" mean?

To eat or drink, and, as a noun, food in general &mdash; Toki Pona doesn't separate eating from drinking the way English does. <b>moku pona</b> is "good food."

## What does "alasa" mean?

To hunt, forage, gather, or try to get something &mdash; the effort of pursuing and obtaining, whether that's an animal, food, or an opportunity. <b>mi alasa e kili</b> is "I gather fruit."

## What does "utala" mean?

To fight, and, as a noun, a fight, conflict, battle, or competition &mdash; any struggle between opposing sides, physical or otherwise. <b>utala li lon</b> is "there's a fight/conflict happening."

## What does "unpa" mean?

Sex, or to have sex &mdash; the plain, clinical word for sexual or intimate activity, used the same matter-of-fact way any other action word is used.
