# 13 Nature and elements

## What does "kon" mean?

Air, breath, wind, gas, or an invisible essence/spirit &mdash; anything present but unseen. <b>kon li tawa</b> is "there's wind" (air moving); it also covers smell and, more abstractly, spirit or soul.

## What does "lete" mean?

Cold, or (of food) raw/uncooked &mdash; the temperature word, applied to weather, objects, or dishes alike. <b>telo lete</b> is "cold water."

## What does "ma" mean?

Land, earth, ground, country, or the outdoors in general. <b>ma Kanata</b> is "the land of Canada"; <b>mi lon ma</b> can mean "I'm outside" or "I'm on the ground."

## What does "mun" mean?

Moon, star, or any celestial body seen in the night sky &mdash; Toki Pona doesn't separate "moon" from "star" the way English does. <b>mun li suli</b> is "the moon/a star is big."

## What does "seli" mean?

Fire, heat, or warmth, and by extension to cook or burn something. <b>telo seli</b> is "hot water"; <b>mi seli e moku</b> is "I cook the food."

## What does "sike" mean?

Circle, sphere, wheel, or cycle &mdash; any round shape or repeating loop, including a year as a cycle of time. <b>sike sin</b>, "new cycle," is one common way to say "new year."

## What does "suno" mean?

Sun, light, or brightness. <b>suno li lon</b> is "it's daytime/sunny" (literally "the sun is present").

## What does "telo" mean?

Water, or any liquid. <b>mi moku e telo</b> is "I drink water"; as a modifier, <b>telo</b> marks something as wet or liquid.

## Translate into Toki Pona: "The water is cold."

<b>telo li lete.</b>
