# 14 Living things

## What does "soweli" mean?

A land mammal &mdash; and, loosely, "animal" in general when the exact kind doesn't matter. <b>soweli</b> covers everything from a mouse to a horse.

## What does "waso" mean?

Bird, or any flying creature. <b>waso li tawa sewi</b> is "the bird flies upward."

## What does "kala" mean?

Fish, or any sea creature &mdash; whales, octopuses, and dolphins fall under it just as easily as fish do.

## What does "akesi" mean?

Reptile or amphibian &mdash; the broad category for lizards, snakes, frogs, and similar creatures that don't fit <b>soweli</b> or <b>waso</b>.

## What does "pipi" mean?

Bug or insect &mdash; any small crawling or flying creature in that general size and shape, spiders included even though they're not technically insects.

## What does "kasi" mean?

Plant, herb, leaf, or vegetation in general &mdash; the living plant itself, as opposed to something harvested from it. <b>kasi li kule</b> is "the plant is colorful."

## What does "kili" mean?

Fruit, vegetable, mushroom, or other edible produce. It often refers to something gathered or harvested for food, but it need not be a part of a plant: mushrooms are fungi. <b>mi moku e kili</b> is "I eat fruit, vegetables, or other produce."

## What does "pan" mean?

Grain, bread, pasta, or any cereal-based food &mdash; the specific edible category kili doesn't cover. <b>pan</b> is what a loaf, a bowl of rice, or a plate of noodles all have in common.

## Translate: "kili ni li pona tawa mi."

"I like this fruit, vegetable, mushroom, or other produce" &mdash; more literally, "this produce is good to me." Context supplies the specific kind of <b>kili</b>.
