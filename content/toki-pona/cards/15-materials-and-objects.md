# 15 Materials and objects

## What does "ilo" mean?

Tool, device, or machine &mdash; anything built or used to do a job. <b>ilo sitelen</b> would be "a writing/drawing tool," a pen or a computer alike &mdash; a single-word modifier needs no <b>pi</b>.

## What does "kiwen" mean?

A hard object &mdash; stone, rock, or metal. <b>kiwen</b> names the material property (hardness) as much as any specific substance.

## What does "ko" mean?

A soft or semi-solid substance &mdash; paste, powder, dough, mud, clay, or snow. Where <b>kiwen</b> is hard, <b>ko</b> is squishable, and both describe a texture more than one specific material.

## What does "len" mean?

Cloth or clothing &mdash; fabric in general, and anything made from it worn on the body. <b>len mi</b> is "my clothes."

## What does "linja" mean?

A long, flexible thing &mdash; string, rope, thread, or hair. <b>linja lawa</b> ("head-string") is a common way to say "hair."

## What does "lupa" mean?

A hole, doorway, or window &mdash; any opening in a surface. <b>lupa tomo</b> could be a house's door or its window, depending on which opening is meant.

## What does "nena" mean?

A bump, hill, mountain, or protrusion &mdash; and by extension a button, since it physically bumps out. <b>nena suli</b> is "a big hill/mountain."

## What does "palisa" mean?

A long, rigid thing &mdash; a stick, rod, or pole. Where <b>linja</b> bends, <b>palisa</b> stays straight and stiff.

## Translate into Toki Pona: "The tool is inside the house."

<b>ilo li lon insa tomo.</b>
