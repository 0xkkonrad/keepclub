# 11 Mind and senses

## What does "ken" mean?

Can, possible, or allowed &mdash; ability or permission, both handled by the same word. <b>mi ken tawa</b> is "I can go" (able to) or "I may go" (permitted to), with context sorting out which.

## What does "kute" mean?

To hear or listen, and by extension the ear itself. <b>mi kute e ona</b> is "I hear/listen to them."

## What does "lukin" mean?

To see, look, or watch &mdash; and by extension sight or a glance. <b>mi lukin e ni</b> is "I see/look at this."

## What does "musi" mean?

Fun, play, game, or art &mdash; anything entertaining, and the act of playing or having fun. <b>ni li musi</b> is "this is fun"; <b>mi musi</b> is "I'm playing/having fun."

## What does "pilin" mean?

To feel, an emotion, or the heart as the seat of feeling &mdash; both the physical sensation and the emotional one. <b>pilin mi li pona</b> is "I feel good" (literally "my feeling is good").

## What does "sona" mean?

To know, knowledge, wisdom, or a skill. <b>mi sona e ni</b> is "I know this"; <b>mi sona pali</b> is roughly "I know how to work/act," using it as a modifier for "skilled at."

## What does "toki" mean?

Language, speech, or to talk/speak/communicate &mdash; the same word that names the course itself. <b>mi toki e ni</b> is "I say this"; <b>toki mi</b> is "my language."

## What does "wile" mean?

To want, need, wish, or should &mdash; desire and obligation share this one word. <b>mi wile moku</b> is "I want to eat"; <b>sina wile pona</b> can mean "you should be good" as much as "you want to be good."
